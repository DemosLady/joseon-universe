IN PROGRESS

The palace killed the people, and the people hated the palace. But what came from the north does not tell them apart. Now both sides stand in the same line.

A man who spent his whole life running is told, for the first time, to stand still. The man beside him is a king's soldier and he is a pardoned thief, but today they are both just a line. Two shaking hands level the same spear.

The drum sounds and the line is pushed back. But it does not break. It was not him who held — it was the man beside him.

On the morning after the fighting the king comes to the front. The pardon will be kept, he says. And a man walks out of the line — carrying inside his coat a ledger the sea kept for ten years. The only person in the world who could take it is standing two steps away.

Behind them, a woman who can write is doing something else. When the paper runs out she writes on cloth, and then on wood, and finally on the arm of a living man — so that if he dies, someone will look at that arm and read. Not the number the army gave him. The name they called him at home.

And on the day the fighting ends, a rider comes from the capital with a sealed decree in his hand. Nobody opens it. Nobody has to open it to know.
