The first chapter of the Iron Trilogy. Fire burns, salt preserves, and iron remembers.

IN PROGRESS

The truth raised from the sea was on its way to the capital. Three days' sailing. That was when the Jurchen crossed the river.

Three fortresses fall in three days, refugees come over the passes, and the state pardons its criminals. Anyone who can fight will not be asked about his past — the men who spent their whole lives running are called on now.

Anchored in a harbour for supplies, the smuggler hears the news and lowers his sail. The writing has waited ten years, he says; it can wait one more day. But the people coming over that pass cannot wait for tonight.

The blacksmith melts down everything the villages bring him. A bell from a temple, a prisoner's chains, a pot that cooked rice, the iron from the handle of some scholar's brush. He does not ask what it was. If it is iron, it will do.

And to a woman past forty, that winter, the ledger from the sea and the war arrive in the same week. She walks north carrying paper and ink — to write the names of the dying on the day they die.

The palace killed the people, and the people hated the palace. But what came from the north does not tell them apart.
