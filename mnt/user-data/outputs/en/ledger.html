The ledger

NINE HANDS · SIX HUNDRED YEARS

Nobody in this story sets out to save it. Each person holds it for a while and hands it on, and the longest it is ever safe is when it is buried in salt and nobody remembers where.

  1. 01

    A clerk's handone year

    Somebody wrote this ledger. The names of the men who bought false testimony, what they paid, and the state's seal on it. Whose hand that was is never revealed in the story.

    CH. 4

  2. 02

    The seaten years

    The ship goes down on the northern shoal. The ledger sits inside the broken hull in salt water for ten years. The salt eats half the paper and preserves the other half.

    CH. 5

  3. 03

    Ok-ihalf a day

    The diver goes down without her abalone knife — today she is not going to take, but to receive. She brings it up onto the boat, and does not go ashore with it.

    CH. 5

  4. 04

    Su-gyeomtwo months

    They needed someone who could read. The exiled scholar spreads the soaked paper out and reads for three days. And he learns why he was sent to this island.

    CH. 5

  5. 05

    Bae-swea little over a year

    The smuggler carries it north inside his coat. It is in his coat on the battlefield. It is in his coat in front of the king. Two steps would have been enough — and he took his hand out empty.

    CH. 8

  6. 06

    Bun-ione day

    He gives it to her. Asked how long she will keep it, she says: until there is someone left to ask. And she passes it on within a day.

    CH. 8

  7. 07

    The soldiersixty years

    Because he is twenty. Because it looks like he will live a long time. Do not ask what it is, she says. He could not read it — and because he could not read it, he kept it.

    CH. 9

  8. 08

    The salt jarfive hundred years

    His mother's kitchen. He takes out half the salt, lays the oiled cloth underneath, and covers it with salt again. Long after the house is gone, and the kitchen, and the field — the jar is still there.

    CH. 9

  9. 09

    A gloved handtoday

    Someone digs where that kitchen stood. A hand stops above the paper — five hundred years ago a hand stopped here too. That time it came out empty, and that was right. Today is different.

    CH. 9
