TRILOGY ONE

The Fire Trilogy

CHAPTER ONE

Blood and Fog

A woman dies in the palace. Her name is in no record and her grave is unmarked. The king who loved her discovers that the hand which poisoned her belonged to his own court.

From that day he signs his decrees in blood instead of ink. The gentle king became a tyrant.

CHAPTER TWO

Ink and Embers

A scholar named Yun-je submits a petition and is exiled to Jeju. He keeps writing there, and his words cross back to the mainland as the songs of a market clown named Tal-swe.

Jeong-an, a woman who lost her family in a purge and took vows on a mountain, comes down carrying a torch. That night beacons rose on every pass. The uprising did not last half a day.

CHAPTER THREE

Ash and Seed

Yun-je dies at the execution ground. Tal-swe throws away his mask and walks the roads as a storyteller. Jeong-an returns to her mountain empty-handed.

Forty years later, a sixteen-year-old girl named Bun-i finds a sheet of paper beneath the floorboards of her grandmother's room. She cannot read. She sits at the teacher's door for three days asking to be taught.

TRILOGY TWO

The Sea Trilogy

CHAPTER FOUR

Salt and Waves

A generation later, four people are on a small southern island: Su-gyeom, another scholar exiled for writing the truth; Bae-swe, a smuggler who raises a black sail only when the moon hides; Gap-su, an old fisherman the sea has taken everything from; and Ok-i, a diver who lowers her head on land and is master beneath the water.

That summer Ok-i sees a sunken black ship on the northern shoal. And tells no one.

Read this chapter →

CHAPTER FIVE

Sea and Bones

A storm turns the sea floor over. In the middle of it Gap-su speaks quietly to the sea — if you have something to take, take me; and if you have something to give back, give it back now.

The next day Bae-swe finds the bow, and Ok-i brings up a chest. Inside are the names of those who bought false testimony. Only now does Su-gyeom understand that he was not exiled for writing the truth — he was sent to the place where the truth was buried.

CHAPTER SIX

Island and Stars

Three men raise a white sail and head north. But halfway across, Ok-i stands up — on the mainland, she says, she is a woman with no letters and no name. And she goes into the water without a sound.

The three find Jeong-an on Songni Mountain, and she reads faces where the ledger has only names. At dawn the hermitage door stood open and there was no one inside. What happened after that is in no record.

TRILOGY THREE

The Iron Trilogy

CHAPTER SEVEN

Snow and Iron

That winter the north is breached. The Jurchen cross the river and three fortresses fall in three days.

Anchored in a harbour for supplies, Bae-swe hears the news and lowers his sail — the writing has waited ten years, he says, it can wait one more day. The state pardons its criminals and calls on the men who spent their lives running. A blacksmith melts down a temple bell.

And that winter, to Bun-i, now past forty, the ledger from the sea and the war arrive in the same week. She walks north carrying paper and ink — to write the names of the dying on the day they die.
